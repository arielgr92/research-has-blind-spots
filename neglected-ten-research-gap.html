Before the stories

Neglect is a system design problem.

A disease can be common yet poorly measured. It can be rare yet devastating, or concentrated in places where markets expect little return. It can have funding—but still lack the diagnostics, trial infrastructure, or lived-experience research people need.

This is not a definitive global ranking of “least funded” diseases. Funding datasets measure different things and often miss private investment. Each section names a particular, documented pathway by which research or care falls short.

Burden ≠ funding

Population counts, disability, mortality, and research dollars answer different questions.

Investment ≠ access

A treatment on paper does not guarantee diagnosis, affordability, or continuity of care.

Data ≠ every story

Under-diagnosis and exclusion make many burdens look smaller than they are.

01Inherited blood
Sickle cell disease

Pain was made
less visible.

Sickle cell disease has a long history of racial inequity in care and research. Pain crises have been doubted or undertreated; care systems and trial access are uneven. The gap is not simply “no money.” It is whether investment reaches pain science, implementation, and the communities carrying the burden.

Scale
~100k

People in the U.S. live with sickle cell disease.

U.S. research contextNIH RCDC reported $153m in FY 2024. RCDC categories overlap and are not global funding totals.
02Invisible illness
ME/CFS

When no test
becomes no proof.

For decades, ME/CFS was minimized and poorly defined. Without a simple diagnostic biomarker, patients may be misclassified and researchers struggle to build comparable cohorts. That creates a feedback loop: weak measurement depresses visibility; low visibility limits the infrastructure needed to improve measurement.

Scale
3.3m

Estimated U.S. adults living with ME/CFS, according to CDC material.

What investment must buildBiomarkers, durable cohorts, meaningful outcomes, and trials designed with people who live with post-exertional symptom worsening.
03Reproductive health
Endometriosis

Pain becomes
“normal.”

Endometriosis shows how gender bias can become a research bottleneck. Symptoms can be normalized or treated as nonspecific, diagnosis is often delayed, and the biology is complex. Delay narrows the evidence base and can leave patients without timely, noninvasive pathways to diagnosis or durable treatment.

Scale
~190m

People of reproductive age affected worldwide, according to WHO.

U.S. research contextNIH RCDC reported $28m in FY 2024. This is an NIH category, not a measure of every country’s or company’s investment.
04Neglected tropical disease
Chagas disease

A quiet disease
in a quiet market.

Chagas disease can remain silent for years before causing heart or digestive complications. It primarily affects populations with less purchasing power and is under-recognized outside endemic settings. That is a market failure: commercial incentives do not naturally reward the diagnostics, treatments, surveillance, and delivery systems a public-health response needs.

Scale
~6–7m

People estimated to be infected worldwide, according to WHO.

What gets missedChronic disease management, congenital transmission, migrant-inclusive screening, and tools suitable for low-resource settings.
05Neglected tropical disease
Schistosomiasis

Medicine alone
is not a system.

Mass treatment saves lives, but it cannot substitute for clean water, sanitation, diagnostics, surveillance, and local delivery capacity. When programme support is short-term or fragmented, the work that prevents reinfection and tracks progress is vulnerable. The research need includes implementation—not just a new pill.

Scale
253.8m

People required preventive treatment globally in 2023.

Why investment stallsNTD programmes are often donor-dependent, while operational funding and diagnostics are less visible than drug donation campaigns.
06Neglected tropical disease
Lymphatic filariasis

Elimination needs
the long view.

Lymphatic filariasis can cause lifelong disability and stigma. Even where transmission falls, people may still need chronic-care services. Success is at risk when funding only rewards the next campaign rather than sustained surveillance, morbidity management, and locally owned health systems.

Scale
657m

People in areas requiring preventive chemotherapy in 2023.

What investment must protectMass drug administration, care for lymphoedema and hydrocele, and the monitoring required to prevent resurgence.
07Inflammatory skin disease
Hidradenitis suppurativa

Stigma hides
the signal.

HS can cause painful lesions, drainage, scarring, and major disruption to daily life. Its body locations and symptoms can make people reluctant to seek care; clinicians may mistake it for a simple infection. When recognition is late, research recruitment, timely referrals, and reliable prevalence estimates all suffer.

Scale
~1%

Commonly cited prevalence estimate; it varies by population and definition.

What is neededFaster diagnosis, inclusive trials, better pain outcomes, and research that treats quality of life as a core endpoint.
08Connective tissue
Ehlers-Danlos syndromes

Fragmented names,
fragmented evidence.

EDS covers several heritable conditions with varied symptoms and genetic causes. Heterogeneity matters: it can make shared diagnostic criteria, natural-history studies, and trial recruitment difficult. “Rare” should never mean “too complicated to study.” It means research networks and meaningful measures must be built deliberately.

Classification
13

EDS subtypes recognized in the 2017 international classification.

What is neededSpecialist networks, longitudinal registries, validated outcomes, and studies that reflect the diversity of EDS experiences.
09Genetic condition
Fragile X syndrome

Small cohorts,
long timelines.

Rare genetic conditions face a structural mismatch: each family’s need is urgent, but recruiting large trials can take years. Outcomes may vary with age and support needs, while specialist care is dispersed. Investment must carry the cost of coordination that a large, common-disease market can often absorb.

Scale
1 in 4,000

Common estimate for males affected; estimates vary by method and population.

What is neededFamily-centered registries, cross-site trials, life-course research, and care models that do not stop at childhood.
10Neurodegeneration
Lewy body dementia

Hard to name,
hard to trial.

Lewy body dementia can involve cognitive changes, movement symptoms, sleep disruption, and hallucinations; it often overlaps with other diagnoses. Diagnostic uncertainty delays entry into trials and complicates the development of biomarkers, while carers can be left navigating multiple systems at once.

Scale
~1.4m

People estimated to live with LBD in the U.S.

U.S. research contextNIH RCDC reported $114m in FY 2024. Needs include accurate diagnosis, inclusive trials, and support for carers.
+1Mental health
Bonus lens: schizophrenia

Burden can be
under-counted.

Schizophrenia is researched and receives significant public investment; it should not be described as universally neglected. Yet stigma, under-detection, and the exclusion of mortality and informal care from some burden estimates can hide its full impact. The sharper claim is a research-and-care gap: advocates argue investment and innovation remain small relative to the disorder’s disability, economic cost, and unmet global treatment need.

Global prevalence
~23.2m

Estimated prevalent cases worldwide in 2021, from GBD-based analysis.

Why this belongs hereEvidence and services are disproportionately concentrated in high-income settings; research and care data from lower-resource regions remain thin.
The machinery behind the gap

How systems turn unmet need into a research blind spot.

01 / MARKET

Expected profit outranks public value.

For diseases concentrated in lower-income settings, limited purchasing power can make conventional, patent-led incentives a poor match for health needs.

02 / MEASUREMENT

What is not counted is easier to defer.

Missing diagnostics, poor surveillance, and narrow outcomes make disease burden—and the payoff from research—appear smaller than lived reality.

03 / STIGMA

Silence weakens the pipeline.

Dismissal of pain, mental-health stigma, and shame around symptoms can delay care and make it harder to form visible, well-supported research communities.

04 / INFRASTRUCTURE

Trials need a home.

Rare, chronic, and geographically dispersed conditions need registries, specialist sites, shared measures, and long-term support before a trial can even begin.

The question to carry forward

Research justice means designing for the people data leaves out.

Ask better questions.

Who was not counted? What outcome was not measured? Which part of the pathway—basic science, diagnosis, clinical trials, delivery, or long-term care—has been asked to survive without stable support?

Those questions can turn attention into accountability.
